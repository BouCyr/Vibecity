# Vibecity

Procedural generator for the plan of a fictional medieval city. Plain HTML/JS, no framework, no build: open `index.html` in a browser.

- Left panel: numbered generation steps, collapsed by default (click a header to open it), each with its own settings.
- Everything is derived from the **seed** (each step gets its own seeded RNG), so the same seed + settings always give the same map.
- Seed and settings are stored in the URL hash, so a map can be shared by URL.
- Map is 2000×2000 units — one unit is 2.5 m (`Units.METRES_PER_UNIT`, `js/units.js`), so the map is 5 km × 5 km and a scale bar sits at the bottom right. It is fitted to the window at first; wheel (or pinch) zooms about the pointer, drag pans, double-click fits the map again.

## Streets page (`rues.html`)

A separate page for the fine generation of streets, built on the same shell (`js/app.js`, configured through `window.VibecityPage`: map size, step list, storage key) with its own steps in `js/rues/`. The map is 500×500 units (1.25 km × 1.25 km).

1. **Voronoi diagram** — 30 random points by default (true random or Poisson disk) and their Voronoi cells, with Lloyd relaxation, drawn with the Delaunay triangulation in blue (links between the sites of neighbouring cells); reuses the main page's points and Voronoi code.
2. **Streets** — the sites become crossroads and the Delaunay links become streets (blue). A cell with exactly one edge on the map border gets a street from its site to the middle of that edge (mauve), where it leaves the map; corner cells, with two border edges, get none. The crossroads nearest the geometric centre of the map is the city centre (red).
3. **City exits** — chooses 2 to 10 exits (default 4) among the ends of the mauve streets. Each exit's straightness is the straight-line distance from the city centre over the length of its shortest path along the streets (1 = perfectly straight). A set of exits scores (1 − w) × mean straightness + w × (smallest distance between two chosen exits / ideal distance), the ideal being the side of a regular polygon of that many exits inscribed in the map's circle; w is the *spread weight* (default 0.5). The set is built greedily from the straightest exit, then improved by swaps. Chosen exits and their paths from the centre are drawn in orange, with their straightness.

## Steps

1. **Random points** — number of points, margin from the edges, distribution (true random or Poisson disk).
2. **Voronoi diagram** — one cell per point, clipped to the map, with neighbor adjacency and shared nodes (Voronoi vertices). Setting: Lloyd relaxation iterations (evens out cell sizes).
3. **Altitude** — altitude in [0, 1] for every cell center and node, from three layers of seeded simplex noise (hills, relief, local bumps), each with a size and a weight.
4. **Sea** — choose the sea fronts (N/E/S/W) and the share of sea cells (default 25%). A reference coast is built from the fronts: for one front, a slanted line drawn from the seed (it leaves the front edge 100–250 px from one corner, picked at random, and reaches the adjacent edge as far inland from the other corner); the same for each of two opposite fronts, drawn independently; a parabola from corner to corner bending toward the shared corner for two adjacent fronts (*Curve bend*: 0 = straight diagonal, 1 = control point on the corner, so the curve starts along the edges; default 0.4); a parabola between the corners of the land edge, with its vertex at the middle of the opposite edge, for three fronts; a centered circle for four. Altitude is lowered by the signed distance to that curve (reach, strength), rescaled to [0, 1], then the lowest cells become sea. Without any sea front there is no sea at all. The curve is drawn dashed on the sea layer.
5. **Sea clean-up** — (a) land cells along a sea-front edge become sea, unless linked through such land cells to the land cell of a corner that is not between two sea fronts; (b) sea areas reaching no sea front are filled up to the lowest surrounding land altitude. (c) small islands (up to 3 land cells) become sea. Sea altitude is then set to 0. Its layer outlines the changed cells (blue: became sea, orange: became land).
6. **Merge short edges** — every Voronoi edge shorter than *Minimum edge length* (default 30 px, 0–100) is removed, shortest first: its two nodes are merged (at the midpoint, or at either end if the midpoint would fold a neighbouring cell; nodes on the map border stay on it, corners stay in place). A triangle cell along the edge collapses and is removed, its two neighbours then sharing an edge. Neighbours, node/cell links and sea flags are rebuilt. A land cell left touching the map border by a single point between sea cells becomes sea, and so do the small islands (up to 3 cells) this may leave. Its layer marks the merged nodes.
7. **Rivers** — a *fleuve* and tributaries, along Voronoi edges between two land cells, chosen by a *climb*. Fleuve: the mouth is the coast node nearest to the map center; every land node gets its shortest distance (px, along land edges) to the coast; among the paths from the mouth to a map border along which that distance strictly increases, the border end is the one reached by the longest; the fleuve then takes the *shortest* such path to it (*Course*: shortest climb, default, fewer meanders; or longest climb). *Meanders* (sine-generated, default; or none): right after each course is found (fleuve first, then each tributary before the next one), it is redrawn along an ideal meandering curve — the classic *sine-generated curve* of rivers like the Seine: along the bed, the angle to the valley axis oscillates as ω·sin(2π s / λ). The valley axis is the course heavily smoothed; *Meander wavelength along the valley* (450 px, the same for the tributaries) and *Meander angle* ω (95°; above ~100° the loops nearly close) set the loops, *Meander irregularity* (0.15) varies both at each half wave; the amplitude ramps up from 0 after the mouth or junction and back down before the source. The course is then snapped onto the Voronoi edges: shortest path where an edge costs its length × (1 + (distance to the curve / 20 px)²), only through nodes near the curve, advancing steadily along it (no shortcut across a loop's neck), avoiding other courses, the coast and the border, ending on the border near the end of the curve. A tributary keeps its meanders only if the confluence angle stays within limits (otherwise gentler loops, then none). The altitude correction below then makes the water flow downstream all along. The ideal curves are drawn dashed on this step's layer. As soon as a course reaches a node joined to the map border by an edge, it takes that edge and ends there. If no such path exists, the next coast nodes by distance to the center are tried. Without any sea front, a drawn exit edge plays the coast's role. Tributaries: same algorithm, the distance being taken to the coast *or* any existing course; every fleuve node more than *First confluence from the mouth* (200 px, along the fleuve) away is tried as a junction and the **longest** valid tributary is kept; the second one is the longest on the other bank, with its junction at least *Gap between confluences* (100 px) from the first one (upstream or downstream). Courses keep apart: every edge of a tributary (and of its meanders) must end at least *Minimum distance between courses* (80 px) from the courses already built, or, while it is still closer (at its start, from its junction), farther from them than the previous node — it moves away, then never comes back. The loops of one course keep the same distance apart: two parts of the ideal curve more than 3 × that distance apart along it must not come closer (the half waves around such a neck are made gentler), and the snapped course only uses nodes that far from the curve's other loops. A junction is valid only if the angle between the two upstream branches (measured at the first point 120 px away in a straight line, also after simulated smoothing) is 75° or less, and more than 25° (checked 40, 80 and 120 px from the junction, so that a branch never runs alongside the other before joining). *Watercourses*: fleuve + 2 tributaries by default (0–3). Altitude: *carve* (altitude made to decrease downstream, valley carved around each course) or *recompute* (from the distances to the coast and to the rivers, keeping part of the step 3 relief). Widths: *Fleuve width* (20 px), *River width* (15 px), and the fleuve gains *Fleuve widening per confluence* (3 px) downstream of each confluence (20 → 23 → 26). Data: `state.rivers` (`kind`, `nodes` from source to mouth/junction, `widths` per node, `edgeWidths` per edge, `joins`, `mouth`), `state.riverEdges` (edge key → river), `node.river`, `cell.riverbank`.
8. **Smooth coast & rivers** — the sea outline and the river courses are resampled every 4 px and smoothed with a Gaussian-like filter (setting: radius in px, capped at 0.6 × the mean cell spacing, ), so no bend is tighter than about that radius. If a cell would fold: a sliver cell made only of smoothed nodes (inside a tight bend) is merged into the cell across its longest other edge; otherwise its free nodes follow the curve, then the cell is merged across the edge next to its most displaced node; only as a last resort is the smoothing relaxed locally or the radius lowered; map-border stretches, river sources, mouths and confluences stay in place. The result is part of the data: coast nodes are moved onto the curve (`node.x/y`), the points between two coast nodes are stored per coast edge (`state.coastEdgePoints`, keyed by node pair) and inserted into the polygons of both cells along that edge (`cell.poly`, with `cell.polyAlt` giving an altitude per polygon point). Cells stay seamless. The cell-based Sea layer (step 4) is hidden by default.
9. **River cells** — runs after the smoothing: every watercourse becomes a band of real water cells of fixed width, built along the samples of its smoothed centre line, and the cells around are pushed aside smoothly. Every polygon point becomes a node; each centre-line sample is split into one copy per side, at exactly half the width from the course (at a confluence, the corner where the banks of two branches meet; at a source on the map border, sliding along the border; at the mouth, square to the course, the sea cell taking the copies). Every other point moves away from each course by (w/2)·(1 − smoothstep(d / *Push falloff*)) (default 60 px; the pushes of all courses add up, points on the map border only slide along it, no point is pushed into a band). Inside bends tighter than the half width the bank folds: the fold is cut. One water cell per course edge of step 7 (`cell.river`), one triangle per confluence (`cell.junction`), `river.cells`, `river.centerline`, `state.shore` (water/land edges), `cell.riverbank`. Every cell is checked (winding, no self-crossing, exact tiling); a faulty one is fixed locally (block move, stronger push, merge into a neighbour), then, as a last resort, the push is made gentler or the course narrowed — the step info says so. When shown, it draws the water instead of steps 7 and 8.

10. **Altitude curve** — last step: every altitude computed so far (cells, nodes, per-point polygon altitudes, water cells) is normalized to [0, 1], mapped through the *Altitude curve*, then multiplied by *Maximum altitude* (10–3000 m, default 300): altitudes are in metres from here on (sea at 0 m). The curve goes from (0, 0) to (1, 1) and is strictly increasing (monotone cubic interpolation through its 5 movable points); in its editor, drag one of the 5 points to move it (it stays strictly between its neighbours); *Reset curve* restores the straight line. Then: (a) the land falls to 0 m at the coast — altitudes are multiplied by smoothstep(distance to the coastline / *Coast slope width*, 200 px), the sea is 0 m; (b) every river gets a water level per centre-line sample, the same on both banks (water is flat across, so the edges crossing the river are level), strictly decreasing downstream, rising upstream by at most *Max river gradient* (10 m per 100 px) and kept below the land around; the fleuve ends at 0 m at its mouth, a tributary at the fleuve's level at their confluence; (c) the land within *Valley width* (150 px) of a river is lowered towards the level of its nearest bank point; (d) any land point touching a river is raised just above the water, and land cell centres above the water of their ring: river nodes are local minima, only their downstream neighbour is lower. The Altitude layer (step 3) shows the result.

11. **Raster fields** — the cell data sampled on a regular grid (`js/fields.js`), the form every city rule reads: *Pitch* (map units per sample, default 2 = 5 m, so 1000 × 1000 samples); the layer shows one field as an image (*Field shown*: altitude, water, distance to the water with a band every 250 m, slope). Fields: `altitude` (metres; sea 0, every other cell interpolated as a fan of triangles from its centre to its polygon points, like the 3D view — river cells thus follow the water level of their banks), `water` (1 on sea and river cells), `sea`, `river`, `fleuve` (the same for the sea cells, the river cells, the cells of the fleuve), `waterDistance`, `seaDistance`, `riverDistance`, `fleuveDistance` (exact Euclidean distance in map units to the nearest such sample, Felzenszwalb–Huttenlocher transform), `slope` (rise over run from central differences). Any step gets one with `Fields.get(state, name, pitch, ctx)`: built on first use, cached in `state.fields` (so rebuilt at every generation), each a `Field` with `w`, `h`, `pitch`, `data` (`Float32Array`), `at(x, y)` (bilinear), `cell(x, y)`, `map(fn)`, `minMax()`. `Fields.rasterizeCells`, `Fields.distanceTo` and `Fields.slopeOf` build custom ones.

12. **3D view** — optional display mode (WebGL, no library): tick its box and the map is shown as a relief instead of the 2D layers. Each cell is a fan of triangles from its centre to its polygon points at their altitudes; land coloured like the Altitude layer, water (sea, river cells) blue; the sea is flat at 0 m and vertical cliffs close the gap where the coast is higher. Lighting: ambient + diffuse (Lambert, smooth normals) from a directional light. Settings: *Vertical scale* (px per metre, default 1), *Ambient light* (0.35), *Light direction* (azimuth, 0 = north) and *Light elevation*. Drag to orbit, right- or shift-drag to pan, wheel (or pinch) to zoom.

13. **Fine terrain** — the altitude known per cell (~110 m apart, faceted) becomes a metre-scale raster, `state.terrain` (also the `terrain` and `terrainSlope` fields): the facets are smoothed on the land only (*Smoothing radius*, 100 m; a normalized blur, so the water does not pull the banks down), three octaves of simplex noise (wavelengths 150, 50 and 17 m) add *Detail amplitude* (4 m) that fades within 25 m of the water and on the flat, and the banks (land within 75 m of the water) stay 0.3 m above the level of the nearest water sample (`state.waterLevel`). Land within 400 m of the water, less than *Marsh height* (1.5 m) above it and nearly flat is marsh (`state.marsh`, patches after a 50 m blur). The layer draws a hillshade (lit from the north-west) and the marsh.

14. **Site** — where the city goes. Every point of the map (a candidate every 8 units = 20 m, on land, at least *Minimum distance from the map edge* — 1500 m — from the border so that the countryside fits around) is scored from the raster fields for a medieval town and the best becomes the centre, `state.site` (`x`, `y`, `score`, `terms`, `radius`, `toWater` unit vector, `waterPoint` the nearest point of the watercourse, `axis` angle of the course). Criteria, each 0–1, over the disc of *City radius* (500 m): *bank* — 100–300 m from the watercourse the town settles on (the fleuve, else any river, else the sea), neither in the floodplain nor far from it; *flat* — mean slope over the disc (0.25 = 14° counts as steep); *crossing* — land straight across on the far bank; *sea* — 300 m to 1.2 km from the sea (the lowest crossing of the fleuve, not the coast itself); *prominence* — the centre up to 30 m above three quarters of the ring at the city radius; *near* — the river within about 1 km; *confluence* — within 750 m of a junction of step 9. *Preference* weights them: river port (bank 3, flat 2, crossing 1.5, sea 1, prominence 0.5, confluence 0.5), hilltop town (prominence 3, near 1.5, crossing 1, flat 0.5, bank 0.5), confluence (confluence 3, bank 2, flat 1.5, crossing 1); the weighted mean is then scaled down when less than three quarters of the disc is land. The layer draws the site (dashed circle at the city radius, the way down to the water) and, with *Layer shows*, the scores as a heat map (the top half, from the median to the best).

15. **Core and walls** — two successive walls around the site, `state.walls` (`kind` inner/outer, `centre`, `poly`, `area` in ha, `towers`, `gates`), and the core `state.core`. Each wall is a star-shaped polygon of 72 rays enclosing a target area (*Great wall* 60 ha, *Old core wall* 10 ha): along each ray the radius, between 0.8 and 1.2 × the nominal one (itself wobbled by three harmonics with random phases, *Irregularity* 0.15), minimizes a cost — the distance to the nominal radius, twice the slope, minus a bonus for standing above a drop (the ground falling 5 m within the next 15 units); the radii are smoothed around the circle, capped by *Setback from the water* (30 m: the river is the defence there, the wall runs along the bank) and kept compact (at most 1.4 × the median radius and 1.1 × the nominal one, so a town hemmed in by water stays smaller — the step says so); a bisection on the nominal radius (up to 1.6 ×) reaches the area. The old core is centred a third of the way from the site towards the water (a bridgehead) and kept 10 units inside the great wall. Towers every *Tower spacing* (50 m). Gates on the great wall: the bridge gate towards the water, then up to *Gates* (4) land gates among 24 directions where the wall is not on the bank, ranked by the share of land along the ray to the map border, at least 60° apart. The old wall's gates are set by the roads.

16. **Main roads and bridge** — from every gate of the great wall, a road in to the core and a road out to the map border (towards the neighbouring towns), `state.roads` (`kind` inward/outward, `gate`, `points`), and the bridge `state.bridge` (`a`, `b`, `length`, `gate`). Roads are cheapest paths (A*, 8 neighbours) on a grid of 8 units (20 m): a cell costs 1 + *Slope penalty* (8) × slope, × *Marsh penalty* (2.5) in the marsh; water and the great wall are impassable except on the bridge and within 12 units of a gate; a road already laid costs 0.7, so later roads join earlier ones where it makes sense. The inward roads are laid first; then the whole intra muros is closed (so that an outward road never cuts through the town along the cheap axes and out of another gate) and each outward road goes to the border cell in its gate's direction (within two cells of it; to any border cell only when that one cannot be reached). The bridge is the shortest crossing found from the bridge gate: start points along the bank up to *Bridge search* (250 m) either side, directions within ±25° of the way to the water, cost 3 × the water span + the offset + the way to the water; the outward road of the bridge gate starts from the far bridgehead. Paths are smoothed (Chaikin). Where the inward roads cross the old wall, that wall gets its gates.

17. **Blocks and streets** — the Voronoi pipeline again, at block scale, inside the great wall: `state.blocks` (`poly` the block, `cell` the Voronoi cell, `centre`, `area`), `state.streets` (edges `a`–`b` between two blocks, `kind` main / ring / street, `width`), `state.intraMuros`. Seeds: pairs mirrored across the guide lines first — the great wall (one seed just inside, its mirror just outside, every 0.8 × the wall block size), the old wall and the inward roads (both seeds inside, every 0.9 × the centre block size) — so that the Voronoi edges run exactly along them (the bisector of a mirrored pair is the line itself); then interior seeds by dart throwing, the distance between seeds growing from *Block size at the centre* (70 m) at the core to *Block size at the wall* (110 m). *Lloyd relaxation* (1) moves the interior seeds only. Every cell of an inside seed is clipped by the wall (crossings by bisection on the depth under the star-shaped wall); each of its edges shared with another inside cell is a street — `main` on an inward road (*Main street width*, 9 m), `ring` along the old wall (the mean of both widths), `street` otherwise (*Street width*, 4 m) — and the cell inset by half the width of each of its streets (1.5 units along the wall: the wall walk) is the block; blocks under 60 units² are dropped. The layer fills the intra muros in the street tone and draws the blocks on it; the roads layer no longer draws the inward roads once the blocks exist.

18. **Lots** — every block is cut into strip lots along its streets, `state.lots` (`poly`, `block`, `edge`, `street` kind, `width`, `front` the middle of the frontage, `inward` and `along` unit vectors, `depth`, `zone`). A block edge is a frontage when a street edge runs parallel within half its width; along each frontage the lot widths are drawn around the target — *Frontage at the centre* (5.5 m) to *Frontage at the wall* (8.5 m), by the distance of the block to the core — jittered by *Width irregularity* (0.3) and summing to the edge length. A lot is the part of the block closer to its own street line than to any other edge line of the block, within the lot depth (*Lot depth at the centre* 20 m to *at the wall* 35 m, at most half the block's thickness), between the two lines square to the frontage; a chain of convex half-plane clips, so the lots of a block tile its bands exactly, corner lots are wedges, and the middle of the block is left to yards and gardens. Lots under 4 units² and frontages under 3.5 m are skipped. The layer outlines the lots.

19. **Buildings** — one house per lot, `state.buildings` (`poly`, `lot`, `floors`, `height`, `roof`, `depth`): the front of the lot, the full width of the frontage (party walls), to a depth of *House depth at the centre* (10 m) to *at the wall* (12 m) ± *Depth variation* (0.25), never beyond the lot; *Share of empty lots* (4 %) stay yards. Floors: mostly 3–4 near the core, 1–3 towards the wall (3.2 m each). The roof is a ridge along the street or, for *Share of gables* (35 %), a gable facing it. The layer fills the footprints and, zoomed in past 6 px per unit, draws the ridge lines.

20. **Squares and monuments** — `state.monuments` (`kind` castle / halles / cathedral / church, `poly` the footprint, `block`, `centre`; the castle also has its `keep`), `state.squares` (`kind` market / parvis, `poly`), `state.churchyards` (`poly`, `church`). The castle takes the block nearest the core (its enclosure is the block, the keep a 16 m square in the middle); the market square the nearest unused block on a main street of 0.12–1.2 ha, hollowed, with the halles (30 × 12 m, down to 16 × 8 m) along its long axis when they fit with 7 m around; the cathedral the largest block of the old core that can hold it — *Cathedral length* (70 m), shrunk by 5 m steps down to 40 m, nave width 0.3 and transept 0.6 of the length — the block becoming its close and parvis; then one parish church per *One parish church per* (10 ha) of the walled area, by farthest-point sampling among the blocks of at least 0.15 ha (the next church goes to the block farthest from all churches, stopping under *Minimum distance between churches*, 200 m), *Parish church length* (28 m). Every church is a cross with the nave west–east and a half-round apse at the east end (the chevet to the east), placed at the block's centroid or around it where it fits. A monument's block loses its lots and houses; a parish church only loses those it touches when grown by 5 m (8 m to the west for a small parvis), which become its churchyard. The lots and buildings are replaced by new, filtered lists (earlier steps' data is never altered — the cache relies on it). The layer draws the squares in the street tone, the churchyards in green, the footprints, and names past 1.2 px per unit.

21. **Faubourgs** — outside the gates the town runs on along the roads as ribbons. Along every outward road, from 15 m past the wall (past the bridge for the bridge road) for *Ribbon length* (400 m), strip lots on both sides: frontage from *near the gate* (9 m) to *at the end* (15 m), ± 25 %, *Lot depth* (45 m) ± 30 %, every lot built in the *Fully built share* (0.4) of the ribbon and then with a probability falling to 0 at the end; a lot is refused when it is not convex (a tight bend), on water, within 10 m of the wall, on a slope over 0.3, near the bridge or within 10 m of another road (the step counts the refusals). Each lot gets a detached house at the front — a share of the frontage from 95 % near the gate to 50 % at the end, 8–11 m deep, 1–2 floors. The lots and houses join `state.lots` and `state.buildings` (new lists, `lot.faubourg` true, `lot.street` 'road'). Then `state.extraMuros`: the *quay* (kind quay) along the bank at the foot of the bridge, 50 m either way, 25 m wide; two *mills* on the bank downstream of the bridge (150 m on the town's side, 300 m on the other), their wheel in the water; the *abbey* (enclosure 120 × 100 m, a 45 m church with the chevet to the east, a 28 m cloister south of the nave) 60–220 m past the end of the longest ribbon, 90 m off the road; the *leper house* (36 × 30 m, a chapel) 250–410 m past the end of another ribbon. The layer draws them and their names past 1.2 px per unit.

22. **Villages and lanes** — the countryside's settlements, `state.villages` (`x`, `y`, `kind` village / hamlet, `lane`, `church`, `houses`), `state.lanes` (`points`, `kind`, `from`, `ford`), `state.villageChurches`. Sites are scored every 20 units: on land, 1–2.8 km from the town's centre, 300 m from the map border, 200 m from the wall, 250 m from the abbey and the leper house, not in the marsh — 1.5 × flat (mean slope over a 60 m disc, all on land) + a stream nearby (plateau of the water distance, 20–75 m ideal, none past 1.5 km) + a road nearby (within 1 km), plus a little noise; *Villages* (4) are picked best first at least *Minimum distance between villages* (900 m) apart, then *Hamlets* (6) at least 500 m from everything. Each is joined to the road network (the outward roads and the bridge, then the lanes already laid) by a cheapest path on the roads' 20 m grid — slope and marsh cost, the town is closed, the sea impassable, a river fordable at 25 per unit, roads and lanes 0.5 — and the lane is smoothed. The houses line the lane as a ribbon (the faubourgs' `layRibbon`): *Village street length* (160 m) for a village with a 20 m church beside the lane 15 m in (chevet to the east), 70 m for a hamlet; the lots and houses join `state.lots` and `state.buildings`. The layer draws the lanes and the village churches.

23. **Fields and woods** — the land around, as a raster of land use every 4 units (10 m), `state.landUse` (`Field` of class ids: water, built, marsh, wood, garden, orchard, vineyard, meadow, heath, arable, road) and a parcel drawing. Settlements (the town within 10 m of its wall, the lots outside it, the villages and hamlets, the abbey and the leper house) get a distance field; roads and lanes are marked. Classes, in order: water; built; road; marsh; wood on slopes over 0.4; garden or orchard (by noise) within 230 m of the town's settlements, 90 m of the others, ± 60 m of noise; vineyard on south-facing slopes (over 6 %, under 35 %) within 1 km of the town; meadow within 50 m of the water on the flat; wood beyond *Woods beyond* (1300 m ± 350 m of noise) from any settlement; on slopes over 0.22 wood or heath; copses by noise (*Copses*, 0.3); arable otherwise. Parcels: furlongs are the Voronoi cells of seeds every *Furlong size* (220 m, jittered; 55 % of that for the bocage); their boundaries are drawn where they cross arable, meadow, vineyard or heath (`state.fieldEdges`); in the open-field system each furlong is filled with parallel strips *Strip width* (14 m ± 30 %) apart, square to the nearest lane when one is within 150 m, else along the contour, drawn over arable land only (`state.fieldLines`); the bocage draws its parcel boundaries as hedges instead. The layer paints the land use (translucent, over the hillshade) and, past 0.45 px per unit, the strips and boundaries.

24. **Drawing** — the plan itself, a pen-and-ink drawing of everything, shown instead of the working layers while its box is ticked (`exclusive`). It is written against a pen (`js/pen.js`: `CanvasPen` for the screen, widths and text sizes in screen px, with viewport culling; `SvgPen` for the export, the whole map at one unit per px), so *Export SVG* writes the same drawing to a file (seed name, ~8 MB). In order: paper, the hillshade and land tints (woods, meadows, marsh, gardens, orchards, vines, heath); contour lines from the fine terrain every *Contour interval* (10 m) with an index line every *Index contour* (50 m), by marching squares on every other sample, land only; the water with its shore and three ripple lines (isolines of the distance to land in the water); the parcels (strips and furlong boundaries, hedges for a bocage) past 0.45 px per unit; textures past 0.8 (tree rings on *Tree symbols density* (0.45) of the wood cells, jittered; dots on the orchards; dashes on the vines; tufts on the marsh); lanes dashed, roads as a double line, the bridge; the town — the street bed, the squares and churchyards, the blocks as a dark mass when zoomed out, else every house in ink (lots as hairlines past 2.5), the monuments, the abbey, mills and quay, the walls with towers and gates; the names (`js/names.js`, from the seed: the town in capitals above its wall, villages, hamlets in italics when zoomed in, the fleuve along its course, the sea in the widest open water, the abbey, the monuments when zoomed in); a cartouche (title, "vers 1400", the number of houses as feux, a 500 m scale bar) and a compass rose, in map units so they scale with the map. `state.render` holds the prepared data (contour segments, ripples, water polygons, texture marks, names).

Each step's checkbox toggles whether its layer is drawn.

Setting types: `range`, `select`, `toggles` (array of selected values), `curve` (array of intermediate `[x, y]` points of an increasing curve), `button` (`action`: the name of a step method called with `(state, settings, ctx)`; a returned string is offered as a file to save). A step marked `exclusive: true` replaces every other layer while shown. `draw` also gets `ctx.viewRect`, the part of the map on screen, for culling. The 3D view builds its mesh on first display only. Generation is cached step by step: after every step from step 10 on, a shallow snapshot of the state is kept, and when a setting changes the generation resumes from the snapshot before the first changed step (the status says so; hover it for the time of every step). This relies on a rule: a step from step 10 on adds keys to the state (and may fill `state.fields` with fields that depend only on earlier steps) but never alters what earlier steps built — the terrain steps 1–9 do alter cells and nodes in place, so a change there regenerates everything.

Add a step by creating `js/steps/<name>.js` exposing `{ id, title, settings, run(state, settings, rng, ctx), draw(g, state, ctx), defaultVisible?, zIndex? }` and adding it to `STEPS` in `js/app.js`. In `draw`, `ctx.px` is the number of map units per screen pixel (`ctx.scale` its inverse): outlines, markers and dashes are sized in multiples of it so they keep the same width on screen at every zoom, while real widths (rivers) stay in map units.

To get a single self-contained HTML file: `python3 tools/build-single.py vibecity.html`.

To fetch reference documents (old city plans…) from Gallica (BnF): `python3 tools/gallica-iiif.py search plan Carcassonne --type carte`, then `python3 tools/gallica-iiif.py {info|manifest|image|tiles} ARK` (standard library only; `--stitch` uses Pillow; see the script's docstring).
