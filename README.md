# Vibecity

Procedural generator for the plan of a fictional medieval city. Plain HTML/JS, no framework, no build: open `index.html` in a browser.

- Left panel: numbered, collapsible generation steps, each with its own settings.
- Everything is derived from the **seed** (each step gets its own seeded RNG), so the same seed + settings always give the same map.
- Seed and settings are stored in the URL hash, so a map can be shared by URL.
- Map is 2000×2000 units, scaled to fit the window.

## Steps

1. **Random points** — number of points, margin from the edges, distribution (true random or Poisson disk).
2. **Voronoi diagram** — one cell per point, clipped to the map, with neighbor adjacency and shared nodes (Voronoi vertices). Setting: Lloyd relaxation iterations (evens out cell sizes).
3. **Altitude** — altitude in [0, 1] for every cell center and node, from three layers of seeded simplex noise (hills, relief, local bumps), each with a size and a weight.
4. **Sea** — choose the sea fronts (N/E/S/W) and the share of sea cells (default 25%). A reference coast is built from the fronts: for one front, a slanted line drawn from the seed (it leaves the front edge 100–250 px from one corner, picked at random, and reaches the adjacent edge as far inland from the other corner); the same for each of two opposite fronts, drawn independently; a parabola from corner to corner bending toward the shared corner for two adjacent fronts (*Curve bend*: 0 = straight diagonal, 1 = control point on the corner, so the curve starts along the edges; default 0.4); a parabola between the corners of the land edge, with its vertex at the middle of the opposite edge, for three fronts; a centered circle for four. Altitude is lowered by the signed distance to that curve (reach, strength), rescaled to [0, 1], then the lowest cells become sea. Without any sea front there is no sea at all. The curve is drawn dashed on the sea layer.
5. **Sea clean-up** — (a) land cells along a sea-front edge become sea, unless linked through such land cells to the land cell of a corner that is not between two sea fronts; (b) sea areas reaching no sea front are filled up to the lowest surrounding land altitude. (c) islands of a single land cell become sea. Sea altitude is then set to 0. Its layer outlines the changed cells (blue: became sea, orange: became land).
6. **Merge short edges** — every Voronoi edge shorter than *Minimum edge length* (default 30 px, 0–100) is removed, shortest first: its two nodes are merged (at the midpoint, or at either end if the midpoint would fold a neighbouring cell; nodes on the map border stay on it, corners stay in place). A triangle cell along the edge collapses and is removed, its two neighbours then sharing an edge. Neighbours, node/cell links and sea flags are rebuilt. Its layer marks the merged nodes.
7. **Rivers** — a *fleuve* and tributaries, along Voronoi edges between two land cells, chosen by a *longest climb*. Fleuve: the mouth is the coast node nearest to the map center; every land node gets its shortest distance (px, along land edges) to the coast; the fleuve is the longest path from the mouth to a map border along which that distance strictly increases. As soon as a course reaches a node joined to the map border by an edge, it takes that edge and ends there. If no such path exists, the next coast nodes by distance to the center are tried. Without any sea front, a drawn exit edge plays the coast's role. Tributaries: same algorithm, the distance being taken to the coast *or* any existing course; every fleuve node more than *First confluence from the mouth* (100 px, along the fleuve) away is tried as a junction and the **longest** valid tributary is kept; the second one is the longest on the other bank, with its junction at least *Gap between confluences* (50 px) from the first one (upstream or downstream). A junction is valid only if the angle between the two upstream branches (measured at the first point 120 px away in a straight line, also after simulated smoothing) is 90° or less. *Watercourses*: fleuve + 2 tributaries by default (0–3). Altitude: *carve* (altitude made to decrease downstream, valley carved around each course) or *recompute* (from the distances to the coast and to the rivers, keeping part of the step 3 relief). Data: `state.rivers` (`kind`, `nodes` from source to mouth/junction, `widths`, `joins`, `mouth`), `state.riverEdges` (edge key → river), `node.river`, `cell.riverbank`.
8. **Smooth coast & rivers** — the sea outline and the river courses are resampled every 4 px and smoothed with a Gaussian-like filter (setting: radius in px, capped at 0.6 × the mean cell spacing, and lowered further if a cell would fold or the cells would stop tiling the map), so no bend is tighter than about that radius; map-border stretches, river sources, mouths and confluences stay in place. The result is part of the data: coast nodes are moved onto the curve (`node.x/y`), the points between two coast nodes are stored per coast edge (`state.coastEdgePoints`, keyed by node pair) and inserted into the polygons of both cells along that edge (`cell.poly`, with `cell.polyAlt` giving an altitude per polygon point). Cells stay seamless. The cell-based Sea layer (step 4) is hidden by default.

Each step's checkbox toggles whether its layer is drawn.

Add a step by creating `js/steps/<name>.js` exposing `{ id, title, settings, run(state, settings, rng, ctx), draw(g, state, ctx), defaultVisible?, zIndex? }` and adding it to `STEPS` in `js/app.js`.

To get a single self-contained HTML file: `python3 tools/build-single.py vibecity.html`.
